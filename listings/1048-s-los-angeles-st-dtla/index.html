The property

A rare mixed-use retail and office asset on a signalized corner in the heart of the Downtown Los Angeles Fashion District. Fifteen thousand square feet, fully leased, and extensively rebuilt — with the kind of in-place rents that make an underwriter sit up.

Following a 2009 fire, the building was substantially reconstructed and modernized: structural repairs, new roof systems, electrical upgrades, plumbing replacement, HVAC installation, elevator replacement, restroom reconstruction, and building-wide improvements. Plans, permits, and final inspections are available for review.

The property is 100% occupied by long-term tenants operating primarily on month-to-month agreements. Ownership has generally not pushed significant rent increases for approximately ten years — which is the whole thesis. In-place income is stable; market rent is somewhere well above it.

That combination — stable current cash flow, a heavily capitalized building, owner-user flexibility, and genuine mark-to-market upside — is what makes this a value-add play rather than a yield purchase. You are buying the spread between what the rent roll says today and what the Fashion District says it should say.

The Fashion District investment case

Downtown Los Angeles' Fashion District is one of the most heavily trafficked retail corridors in Southern California, and signalized corner positions within it are finite. This asset combines that location with something rarer: a building that has already absorbed its capital expenditure.

The economics are unusual in a good way. You acquire stable, fully-leased in-place income at a 5.08% cap — while inheriting roughly a decade of deferred rent growth. Because the tenancy is largely month-to-month, the path to market rent does not require waiting out long lease terms, and an owner-user can take space on their own timeline.

That combination — current cash flow, completed capital work, structural flexibility, and a documented gap between in-place and market rents — is what separates a value-add opportunity from a yield purchase. The upside here is not speculative; it is arithmetic.
