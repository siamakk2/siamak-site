AI consultant vs full-service AI agency

An honest comparison, written by one side of it. Including the cases where you should hire the agency instead — because recommending the wrong fit costs both of us more than losing the job.

The short answer

Hire a full-service AI agency when you need a large team — custom software built from scratch, round-the-clock support, or content at very high volume. Hire a senior-led AI consultancy when what you need most is the right strategy and an accountable team that carries it out at a known price. Siamak Kalhor Consulting is the second kind, and it is not advisory-only: it sets the strategy and does the work.

There are three models, not two

Most comparisons pit “consultant” against “agency” as if those were the only options. In practice you are choosing between three:

The advisor. Tells you what to do, then hands you a document. Useful if you already have a team to execute; frustrating if you don’t.

The full-service agency. Departments for strategy, design, development, content and support, coordinated by an account manager. Built for scale and breadth.

The senior-led builder. One experienced principal who sets the strategy and directs a small team doing the work, at fixed prices. This is where Siamak Kalhor Consulting sits — an AI consultancy that also runs a done-for-you AI agency.

When a full-service agency is the right call

Hire the agency, not us, if any of these is true:

  • You need a 24/7 support commitment with contractual response times.
  • You are building an enterprise platform or proprietary model from scratch that needs a standing development team.
  • You need brand identity design or paid-ads management run in-house — both are outside this practice’s scope and are referred elsewhere.
  • You need very high content volume across many channels and languages every month.
  • Your procurement process requires a vendor above a certain headcount.

When a senior-led consultancy is the better fit

  • You are a business owner, not a department, and you want one accountable person.
  • You want AI deployed safely, with every customer-facing answer approved before launch.
  • You want to know the price before the first meeting, not after three.
  • Your problem is strategy first — positioning, how AI assistants describe you, where leads are lost — and tactics second.
  • You would rather pay for 40 years of judgment than for layers of coordination.

Five questions to ask either one

  1. Who will do the work day to day, and will I ever speak to them?
  2. What did an AI assistant last say about one of your clients, and which sources did it rely on?
  3. Is the price fixed and published, or quoted after discovery?
  4. If we part ways, who owns the accounts, code and content?
  5. How will you measure results in AI answers, where no console exists? (Anyone guaranteeing placement in AI answers is telling you something about the rest of their claims.)
