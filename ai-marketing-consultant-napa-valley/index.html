The short version

Four things worth knowing before you spend anything

  • Napa runs on appointments, and appointments get booked two to eight weeks out. Your website is being read long before anyone contacts you.
  • The facts that decide a booking — price, duration, appointment policy, group size, accessibility, what you actually pour — are the exact facts most tasting room sites omit or bury in a PDF.
  • A model cannot read an image or a PDF. If your tasting menu is a graphic, it is invisible, and you will not be recommended for any wine on it.
  • The visitation numbers circulating in the trade press are being misread. The sharpest declines are national reservation data, not Napa foot traffic.
Discovery

How visitors actually plan a Napa trip now

Trip planning is the single most AI-disrupted category of search there is. Research on real browsing behaviour finds AI summaries appearing on a small minority of one- or two-word searches but a majority of ten-word-plus questions — and nobody plans a wine trip in two words. They ask for a winery that takes walk-ins on a Tuesday, somewhere quiet enough to propose, a producer doing something serious with Cabernet Franc, a tasting a group of ten can actually book, somewhere with shade in August.

Your tasting room staff answer those questions every day. Almost none of the answers exist in writing anywhere on your website. That gap — not your photography, not your rankings — is usually why an assistant names the estate down the road instead.

Longer version: how Napa wineries get recommended by AI and what a tasting room should put on its website.

Wine ecommerce

Where the DTC side leaks

Most valley DTC problems are not platform problems. Whether you run Commerce7, Vinoshipper, eCellar, Shopify or something older, the same three things decide whether you get found and whether the visit converts — and none of them are features you can buy.

  • Product data that reads as data. Varietal, appellation, vintage, production volume, tasting notes and allocation status as structured text a model can retrieve — not as an image of a shelf-talker.
  • Club terms stated plainly. Shipment frequency, cost per shipment, commitment length, cancellation policy, shipping states. Club pages are where wineries hedge most and where prospective members most want certainty.
  • Shipping reality, in writing. Which states you ship to and what happens in a heat hold. Ambiguity here loses orders at checkout and gives assistants nothing to state.

A platform migration is a good moment to fix all three, because the content has to be touched anyway. It is not a reason to migrate on its own.

Measurement

How you would know if any of this worked

There is no Search Console for AI answers, and anyone quoting you a single authoritative "AI visibility score" is quoting an estimate. What actually works is unglamorous: agree the twenty or thirty questions a real visitor would ask before booking, run them across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity, record verbatim whether you are named and whether what is said is accurate, and re-run the identical set later against a stored baseline.

It is a sample, not a census. Answers vary between runs, so each prompt gets run more than once and the pattern is reported rather than a single lucky response. The full method and every deliverable is on the AI Authority Audit page.

FAQ

Working with me in Napa Valley

How do visitors find a Napa winery through AI instead of Google?
They ask a long, conversational question — a winery that takes walk-ins on a Tuesday, somewhere that can seat a group of eight, a producer doing something serious with a particular varietal — and receive two or three names with reasons rather than a page of links. Research on real browsing behaviour finds AI summaries appear on a majority of ten-word-plus questions, and nobody plans a wine trip in two words. The shortlist forms before anyone visits a website.
What actually decides whether an AI names my winery?
Specific facts, stated in readable text, that agree everywhere they appear. Whether tastings require an appointment and how far ahead you book out, what a tasting costs and how long it takes, what you pour, your largest group size, accessibility and parking, and what makes a visit here different. Contradiction between your site, Google Business Profile, the visitor bureau and the wine directories lowers a model's confidence in everything about you.
My tasting menu is a PDF. Does that matter?
Yes, more than almost anything else on this list. A PDF or an image of your offerings is effectively invisible — the varietals, pours and pricing cannot be read, indexed or cited. Put the same content in real text on a real page and keep the PDF as a download if you like it. This is usually an afternoon of work with a disproportionate effect.
Do I need to change ecommerce platforms to fix this?
Almost never. Whether you run Commerce7, Vinoshipper, eCellar or Shopify, the things that decide discovery and conversion are product data written as retrievable text, club terms stated plainly, and shipping states in writing. A migration is a reasonable moment to fix those because the content gets touched anyway — it is not a reason to migrate on its own.
How is this different from hiring a wine marketing agency?
An agency gives you a team and a broad service menu — web design, social, email, ads — usually on a monthly retainer. I am one consultant doing something narrower: establishing what AI systems currently say about your business, why, and what changes it. If you need a site rebuilt or a social calendar run weekly, an agency is the right choice and I will say so. Many wineries need an afternoon of writing rather than a retainer.
Are you actually in Napa Valley?
I live in the valley and my office is in Los Angeles, and the work itself runs online — the audit, the strategy and the execution. So a call in person is easy to arrange, and being local is not a requirement for working together. Clients span California and the rest of the country.
Can you guarantee my winery will appear in AI recommendations?
No, and nobody can. There is no placement to buy inside a generated answer and no ranking to purchase. What can be established is exactly where you stand today across the four major engines, which sources they draw on for your category, what is fixable, and whether the work moved the number when the same prompts are re-run.
