Why Isn't My Advertising Working? A Diagnostic, Not a Pep Talk

8 min read·Siamak Kalhor

If you're reading this because you've spent real money on ads and can't point to what it actually bought you, you're not doing anything unusually wrong. You're doing what most small businesses do: running ads without the infrastructure to know whether they're working. That's not a character flaw. It's a systems problem, and it's fixable — but only if you diagnose it honestly instead of assuming the platform, the budget, or the ad copy is the issue.

Research on small business ad accounts backs up how common this is: small businesses waste roughly a quarter of their PPC budgets due to strategic and managerial errors, not because advertising itself doesn't work. In an audit of small business Google Ads accounts, fewer than half had conversion tracking properly installed — meaning most of those businesses were spending money with no reliable way to know if a single dollar produced a lead, a call, or a sale. You cannot fix a leak you can't see. Before you touch your ad copy or budget, you need visibility.

Start Here: You Might Not Actually Know What's Broken

The single most common reason a business owner says “my advertising isn't working” is that they're judging performance off surface metrics — impressions, clicks, maybe cost-per-click — without ever connecting those numbers to an actual sale. High traffic and low conversions can look identical to no traffic and no conversions from the outside, but they have completely different fixes. If you don't have conversion tracking wired up to real outcomes — a completed purchase, a booked consultation, an actual phone call — you're not seeing the problem. You're seeing noise.

The Four Places Ad Spend Actually Leaks

1. Targeting the wrong audience. This is the most commonly cited cause of wasted spend, and it's the easiest to miss because a campaign can look healthy — decent click-through rate, reasonable cost-per-click — while quietly showing your ad to people who were never going to buy. If your cost-per-click looks fine but your close rate is near zero, the audience is very often the actual problem, not the offer.

2. Landing pages that don't match the ad's promise. An ad's only job is to earn a click. The landing page's job is to close the sale, or at minimum capture the lead — and a huge share of wasted spend happens exactly at this handoff, when someone clicks an ad promising one thing and lands on a page that doesn't clearly and immediately deliver on it. Even a strong ad can't overcome a landing page that confuses or stalls the visitor in the first five seconds.

3. No tracking, or tracking the wrong things. If you can't tell which specific ad, keyword, or audience segment produced an actual paying customer, you can't do the single most important thing in advertising: cut what isn't working and put more money behind what is. Without that, you're not managing a campaign. You're funding one and hoping.

4. No one is actively watching it. A managed $500 a month will consistently outperform an unmanaged $2,000 a month, because advertising platforms are not “set it and forget it” systems — they're auction-based systems that drift, get more expensive, and start showing your ad to worse audiences the moment nobody's adjusting them. Ad accounts that don't get reviewed regularly are, structurally, ad accounts that waste money, regardless of how good the original setup was.

The Question That Actually Matters

Not “is my advertising working” — that's too vague to act on. The real question is: of every dollar I've spent this month, can I trace where it went and what it produced? If the honest answer is no, that's your actual starting point. Fix visibility before you touch the budget, the platform, or the creative — because right now, changing any of those is a guess, not a fix.

What This Looks Like Done Right

A business with this working properly can answer, specifically: which campaign, which audience, and which landing page produced last month's paying customers, and at what cost per acquisition. Not roughly. Specifically. That level of visibility is what turns advertising from an expense you hope pays off into a system you can confidently scale, because you know exactly which lever to pull next.
