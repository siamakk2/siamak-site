Why Your Business Doesn't Show Up When People Ask ChatGPT for Recommendations

8 min read·Siamak Kalhor

Somewhere this week, a potential customer typed something like “best [your industry] near me” or “who should I hire for [your service]” — not into Google, but into ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude. And your business wasn't in the answer.

This isn't a hypothetical anymore. ChatGPT alone has grown from around 400 million weekly users in early 2025 to roughly 900 million by early 2026. AI-driven referral traffic to U.S. retail sites surged nearly 700% year over year during the most recent holiday season, and visitors who arrive via an AI recommendation convert at more than four times the rate of a typical organic search visitor — because by the time they click through, the AI has already done the convincing.

Traditional search volume is starting to erode as a result. Analysts project meaningful ongoing declines in classic search-engine query volume as AI chatbots and assistants absorb more of that “where should I go” behavior. For forty years, the game was ranking on a search engine results page. That game hasn't disappeared — but a second one has opened up alongside it, and almost nobody in most local and professional-services categories is playing it yet. Citation rates in professional services remain among the lowest of any industry, under 4% of prompts — which means the businesses who move first have an unusually open field.

Why AI Assistants Skip Most Businesses

An AI assistant doesn't “know” your business the way a person does. It generates an answer based on patterns learned from what's written about you — and about your competitors — across the web. If very little exists, or what exists is thin, inconsistent, or indistinguishable from every competitor's boilerplate “About Us” page, there's nothing for the model to confidently recommend you for.

A few structural realities worth understanding:

  • Third-party sources matter more than your own website. Businesses cited by AI assistants are roughly 6.5 times more likely to be cited via a third-party source — a review site, a directory, a news mention, another site's comparison article — than from their own domain. Your website alone, however well built, is not the whole game anymore.
  • Authority compounds. Sites with a strong base of referring domains are meaningfully more likely to be cited than newer or thinner sites. This mirrors something true in traditional SEO for two decades: authority is not claimed, it's accumulated.
  • Direct, specific answers get cited more. Content that states a clear, specific answer in its opening sentences — not buried under three paragraphs of preamble — is measurably more likely to be pulled into an AI-generated response.
  • The platforms are diversifying. ChatGPT still leads AI referral traffic, but Claude posted the fastest growth of any major platform over the past year, and Gemini has climbed past a quarter of the category. Optimizing for a single AI platform no longer covers the addressable audience the way it once did.

What “Being Visible to AI” Actually Requires

This is not a plugin you install or a checkbox you tick. It's the same discipline that has always separated businesses that get chosen from businesses that get overlooked — applied to a new surface.

Say something specific, not something generic. “We provide quality legal services” gives an AI model nothing to differentiate you with. “We handle catastrophic injury cases involving commercial vehicles in [region]” gives it something to match against a specific query.

Build real third-party presence. Reviews, press mentions, directory listings, guest contributions, partner mentions — every one of these is a signal an AI model can pull from when it's deciding who to recommend. This is where authority is engineered rather than left to chance.

Publish content that answers real questions directly. Not content stuffed with keywords for a 2015-era search engine — content that a model could lift a clear, accurate answer from, because you stated the answer plainly.

Keep your information consistent everywhere. Conflicting details about your business across your website, listings, and profiles create exactly the kind of uncertainty that makes a model hedge and recommend a competitor instead.

The Businesses Getting Left Behind

The pattern I'm seeing across ecommerce, legal, and medical clients is consistent: the businesses treating this as urgent are the ones with the least competition doing it right now. Professional services — law, medicine, finance — currently show among the lowest AI-citation rates of any industry, which sounds like a warning until you realize what it actually means: the field is wide open for whoever engineers their authority first.

This has always been the real principle behind good strategy — build the system that makes chasing every new channel unnecessary, because you were positioned correctly before the channel mattered. AI visibility isn't a new discipline bolted onto marketing. It's the same discipline of engineered authority and strategic clarity, aimed at a new set of eyes reading your content.
