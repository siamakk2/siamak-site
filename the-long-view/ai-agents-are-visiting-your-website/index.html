The Long View / AI Visibility

AI agents are visiting your website. Now you can see them.

Siamak KalhorForty years in marketing — 6 minute read

For thirty years, every visitor to your website had a pulse. You built pages for people, measured people, and argued about what people clicked. That assumption broke quietly this autumn, and on 4 November Microsoft put a number on it.

Microsoft Clarity, the free analytics tool already installed on a great many small business websites, now shows which AI agents visit your site, what they try to do there, and where they get stuck. In the same announcement Microsoft introduced Brand Agents, which put a business's products and services in front of people inside Copilot and Bing. In other words, you can now watch the machines arrive and also send one out to represent you.

2 millionweekly AI agent sessions on websites running Clarity by early September 2026 — nearly double the figure three weeks earlier, from Microsoft’s analysis of 5.23 million agent sessions across 230,511 sites.
Why I am writing thisOwners keep asking me whether AI is “taking their traffic.” It is a better question than it sounds, and it finally has a partial answer you can look at yourself.

What Microsoft actually announced

Three things, and they matter in different amounts.

Agent activity. A dashboard showing which AI agents visited, what they did, and where they ran into friction. It became generally available on 29 October. It currently recognises agents from three operators: Google, OpenAI and Anthropic. Agents from anyone else are not counted yet, so treat what you see as a floor, not a total.

Ask Clarity. You type a question about your website in plain English and get an answer backed by recordings of real sessions. It is in closed beta for retailers, with a waitlist. Useful later; not something to plan around today.

Brand Agents. This is the strategic one. Eligible Shopify and WooCommerce stores can make their products discoverable in Copilot and Bing, and Microsoft says eligible WordPress businesses offering local services can let customers find them and book appointments. Microsoft’s documentation describes the product as a beta, and accepting its terms publishes a copy of your catalogue to Microsoft Merchant Center. No price has been published.

For the first time, a mainstream analytics tool treats the AI agent as a visitor in its own right.

Why this matters more than another feature launch

I have seen every shift in how customers find a business: the Yellow Pages, the first websites, Google, social media, the phone in everyone’s pocket. Each one changed who stood between the customer and you. This is the same pattern again.

Until now, being named by an AI assistant was the whole game. A person asked ChatGPT or Copilot for a dentist, a fabric supplier or a lawyer, the assistant named three businesses, and the person clicked through. What Microsoft’s numbers describe is the next step. Increasingly the assistant does not just name you. It sends an agent to your site to read the menu, compare the prices, fill in the form or start the checkout on the customer’s behalf.

That agent does not admire your photography. It looks for facts it can use and a path it can complete. If the price is hidden inside an image, if the booking form fights automation, if the hours only appear in a PDF, the agent leaves, and the customer never learns you were an option.

The uncomfortable partYour bounce-rate report has been quietly mixing people and machines. A page that “performs badly” may be one that agents cannot use rather than one people dislike.

What has not changed

An agent only visits a business that an assistant has already decided is worth considering. The order is still the same: first the AI has to know who you are, then trust what it knows, then send someone — or something — to act.

So the foundation is unchanged. Consistent business details everywhere you are listed. Independent sources that confirm what you say about yourself. Pages that state plainly what you do, where, for whom and at what price. Structured data a machine can read without guessing. None of that is new. What is new is that you can now see the consequences in a report.

What to do this month

1. Install Clarity and look before you change anything. It is free and takes a few minutes. After a couple of weeks you will see whether agents visit at all, which pages they reach and where they quit. Remember it only counts three operators today.

2. Fix the pages agents abandon. Put prices, hours, service areas and policies in plain text on the page, not in images or downloads. Make sure the contact or booking form works without a login and without a puzzle that only a human can solve. These fixes help human visitors as well, which is the best kind of change.

3. If you sell on Shopify or WooCommerce, consider Brand Agents with your eyes open. It is a beta. Read the terms, understand that your catalogue goes to Microsoft Merchant Center, and add the privacy disclosure Microsoft provides. Have whoever handles your legal and privacy matters look at it first. Being early is useful; being careless is not.

4. Keep working on being named. An agent that never gets sent cannot visit. The work that makes AI assistants recommend you is still the work that comes first.

The long view

Every era of marketing has had a gatekeeper. The editor, the directory, the search engine. Now it is an assistant with an agent running its errands. The businesses that do well with each new gatekeeper are rarely the loudest. They are the ones that make themselves easy to understand and easy to deal with.

Microsoft has just handed you a window onto that new visitor. Look through it before your competitors do. Then make your website as easy for a machine to use as you have always tried to make it for a person.

Common questions

What did Microsoft Clarity announce in November 2026?
On 4 November 2026 Microsoft announced three additions to Clarity: agent activity reporting that shows which AI agents visit a website and where they get stuck, Ask Clarity for plain-language questions about site behaviour, and Brand Agents, which make eligible businesses discoverable in Copilot and Bing.

Can I see AI agent traffic on my website?
Yes, with limits. Microsoft Clarity’s agent activity dashboard, generally available since 29 October 2026, reports visits from AI agents operated by Google, OpenAI and Anthropic. Agents from other providers are not yet recognised, so the figures understate total agent traffic.

How much AI agent traffic are websites getting?
Microsoft analysed 5.23 million AI agent sessions across 230,511 websites using Clarity between 13 August and 9 September 2026. Weekly agent sessions nearly doubled in three weeks to almost two million.

What are Microsoft Brand Agents?
Brand Agents make a business’s products or services discoverable inside Microsoft Copilot and Bing. They are available in beta to eligible Shopify and WooCommerce stores, and Microsoft says eligible WordPress businesses offering local services can use them for discovery and bookings. Accepting the terms publishes the store catalogue to Microsoft Merchant Center.

Does a business still need LLMO if AI agents visit its website?
Yes. An agent visits only businesses an AI assistant has already chosen to consider. Large Language Model Optimization is the work of making sure the assistant knows who you are and trusts what it knows, which comes before any agent is sent.
