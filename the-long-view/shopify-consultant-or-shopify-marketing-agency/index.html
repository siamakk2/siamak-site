Shopify consultant or Shopify marketing agency: fix the store first, or buy traffic?

Siamak KalhorForty years in marketing — 6 minute read

Every Shopify owner whose sales have flattened gets the same two kinds of offer. One says, "Your store needs work." The other says, "You need more traffic." Both come from people who sell that exact service.

The order you buy them in matters more than which one you pick.

Two different jobs

A Shopify consultant, or ecommerce consultant, works on the store itself: product pages, speed, apps, navigation, checkout, shipping and returns information, and how the catalogue is structured. The goal is that more of the people who arrive end up buying.

A Shopify marketing agency works on getting people to the store: paid ads, email, social media, influencers and search. The goal is more visitors and more of the right ones.

Both are legitimate. The trouble starts when an owner buys the second to fix a problem caused by the first.

Why the store comes first

Traffic is the most expensive way to avoid a diagnosis. If ten out of every hundred visitors would buy from a well-built page and your page converts two, every extra dollar of ads buys a fifth of what it should.

A marketing agency can't fix that from the ad account. It can only send more people into the same leak. I've written about the usual leaks in Why Your Store Gets Traffic But No Sales: the gap between cart and checkout, mobile pages that are hard to use, and product pages that don't answer the one question the shopper has.

The new question: do AI assistants recommend your products?

Shoppers increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI for a product recommendation before they visit any store. Those assistants recommend what they can read and trust: complete product data, clear descriptions, real reviews and consistent listings across the web.

That work sits closer to the store than to advertising, which is another reason to fix the store first. I cover the details on AI for ecommerce.

How to judge a Shopify consultant

Ask what they will measure before they change anything, and how they'll know a change worked. Ask whether they've run stores themselves or only built them. Ask what they'd leave alone.

The answer you want is specific: these three pages, this checkout step, this test, this result. Be wary of anyone whose first suggestion is a full redesign or a new theme before they've looked at the numbers.

How to judge a Shopify marketing agency

Ask how they report: by revenue and profit, or by clicks and return on ad spend. Ask what they need the store to fix before they spend your money. A good agency will tell you when your store isn't ready for more traffic, even though saying so costs them a sale.

Common questions

What does a Shopify consultant do?
A Shopify consultant improves the store itself so more visitors buy: product pages, speed, apps, navigation, checkout and the information shoppers need before they commit. Good ones measure first and change the few things that matter most, rather than redesigning everything.

Is a Shopify marketing agency worth it?
Yes, once the store converts well. An agency can grow reach through ads, email and social faster than most owners can alone. Before that point, it mostly buys more visitors for a store that loses them.

What's the difference between an ecommerce consultant and a Shopify consultant?
A Shopify consultant specializes in the platform. An ecommerce consultant looks at the whole business behind the store: margins, ad spend, returns, repeat customers and channels beyond your own site. Many people, including me, do both.

How do I know if my store is ready for more traffic?
Look at conversion and checkout abandonment, on mobile especially. If visitors arrive and leave, or start checkouts and don't finish, fix that first. The free website audit is a quick place to start.
