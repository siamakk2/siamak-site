AI automation agency or business automation consultant?

Siamak KalhorForty years in marketing — 5 minute read

Once an owner decides to automate, the next question is who to hire. Search for help and you'll find two kinds of offer: AI automation agencies that build things, and business automation consultants who tell you what to build.

Both are legitimate. They fail in different ways, and knowing how each one fails is the fastest way to choose.

What each one actually sells

A business automation consultant sells diagnosis. They map how work moves through your business, such as enquiries, bookings, quotes, documents and reports, find the repetitive steps, and tell you which ones are worth automating and in what order. The deliverable is a plan.

An AI automation agency sells construction. They build the automations, connect them to your software, and usually charge monthly to run and maintain them. The deliverable is working systems.

The honest comparison
Business automation consultantAI automation agency
You getA ranked plan of what to automateAutomations built and running
Best whenYou're not sure where to startYou know exactly what you want built
Typical costHourly or a fixed diagnosticSetup fee plus a monthly fee
How it failsA good plan nobody buildsA well-built automation of the wrong thing

The last row is the one that matters. A consultant's plan can sit in a drawer. An agency can build something impressive that saves nobody any time, because it automated what was easy to automate rather than what was costing money.

Before you hire either

Do the thinking yourself first. It takes about a week, and I've laid it out in Which parts of my business should I automate first? You'll arrive at any first call knowing where your time goes, which makes every proposal easier to judge.

Then ask every candidate the same five questions.

What would you not automate in my business? Anyone who says "everything can be automated" hasn't run a business.

Where does a person step in? Good automation hands off to a human when the situation needs judgment. Ask to see the hand-off rules.

What happens when it breaks? It will, at some point. Who notices, how fast, and who fixes it?

Do I own it? If you stop paying, what do you keep: the accounts, the data, the workflows?

How will we measure it? Hours saved, response time, enquiries handled. A number and a date.

Why I do both, in that order

I set up my own practice to avoid both failure modes. I start as a consultant: find the work worth automating and design it with the guardrails in place. Then, if you want, I build it through AI automation and AI agents, and monitor it every month. You can stop after the plan. Plenty of clients do, and hand it to their own team.

Some of the building blocks have published prices. A done-for-you AI agent, for example, is $3,500 to set up and $1,000 a month to run and monitor. If you'd like to see what an agent could do before talking to anyone, you can build a free preview in Agent Studio.

For the plan itself, a free 30-minute call is the start, and a $350 paid hour is often enough to rank the first three automations for a small business. Everything else is on pricing.

Common questions

What does a business automation consultant do?
A business automation consultant maps how work moves through a business, finds the repetitive steps that cost the most time or money, and recommends which to automate first and how, including where a person should stay involved. The deliverable is a ranked plan rather than the software itself.

What does an AI automation agency do?
An AI automation agency builds automations using AI and connects them to the software a business already uses, such as its phone system, calendar, CRM and email. Most charge a setup fee and a monthly fee to run and maintain what they build.

Should I hire an AI automation agency or a consultant?
If you don't yet know what to automate, start with a consultant so you don't pay to build the wrong thing. If you know exactly what you want, an agency is the faster route. One accountable person who diagnoses first and then builds avoids both risks.

What questions should I ask before hiring an automation agency?
Ask what they would not automate, where a person steps in, what happens when something breaks, what you own if you stop paying, and how success will be measured.
